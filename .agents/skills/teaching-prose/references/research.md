# Research behind teaching-prose

Each principle in SKILL.md rests on one or more of these findings. Cite them by name and link when explaining a choice.

## Conversational style (personalisation principle)

- Ginns, P., Martin, A. J. and Marsh, H. W. (2013). Designing instructional text in a conversational style: a meta-analysis. *Educational Psychology Review*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-013-9228-0
- Mayer, R. E. (2005). Principles of multimedia learning based on social cues: personalization, voice, and image principles. *Cambridge Handbook of Multimedia Learning*. https://doi.org/10.1017/cbo9780511816819.014 — conversational rather than formal wording improved learning in 10 of 10 experiments, median effect size 1.3.
- Mayer, R. E., Fennell, S., Farmer, L. and Campbell, J. (2004). A personalization effect in multimedia learning. *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 96(2), 389–395. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.96.2.389

## Explicit links between ideas (text cohesion)

- McNamara, D. S., Kintsch, E., Songer, N. B. and Kintsch, W. (1996). Are good texts always better? *Cognition and Instruction*, 14(1). https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1 — readers with little background knowledge learn more from text that states its connections explicitly; experts can cope with gaps.
- O'Reilly, T. and McNamara, D. S. (2007). Reversing the reverse cohesion effect. *Discourse Processes*, 43(2). https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326950dp4302_2 — skilled readers benefit from cohesive text too.

## What makes an explanation work

- Wittwer, J. and Renkl, A. (2008). Why instructional explanations often do not work. *Educational Psychologist*, 43(1), 49–64. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520701756420 — effective explanations adapt to prior knowledge, focus on concepts and principles, and support rather than replace the learner's own thinking.

## Cutting extraneous material, and text with diagrams

- Cromley, J. G. and Chen, R. (2025). A meta-analysis of Richard Mayer's multimedia learning research. *Educational Research Review*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2025.100730 — overall g = 0.37; among the largest effects were removing seductive detail, personalisation, sentence-level coherence and self-explanation; text plus diagrams gave large, consistent effects.

## Concrete before abstract, counts before probabilities

- Fyfe, E. R., McNeil, N. M., Son, J. Y. and Goldstone, R. L. (2014). Concreteness fading in mathematics and science instruction: a systematic review. *Educational Psychology Review*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-014-9249-3
- McDowell, M. and Jacobs, P. (2017). Meta-analysis of the effect of natural frequencies on Bayesian reasoning. *Psychological Bulletin*, 143(12), 1273–1312. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000126 — 24% solved Bayes problems with natural frequencies against 4% with probabilities.

## Retrieval and self-explanation (for the Your turn and Why? prompts)

- Dunlosky, J. et al. (2013). Improving students' learning with effective learning techniques. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1). Practice testing and distributed practice rated high utility.
- Bisra, K. et al. (2018). Inducing self-explanation: a meta-analysis. *Educational Psychology Review*. Self-explanation g ≈ 0.55.
